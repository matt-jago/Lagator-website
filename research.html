Research

Research

We study a wide variety of topics, united by a single aim: understanding molecular mechanisms in order to predict how microbes change. Our work spans experimental and computational disciplines, from synthetic biology and experimental evolution in the lab to biophysical modelling.

01

Bacterial promoters

Promoters set how strongly a gene is expressed. We build biophysical models that predict expression directly from DNA sequence, and use them to ask how promoters evolve.

  • Predicting gene expression levels directly from sequences
  • Dynamics of gene expression and their consequences
  • Role of promoter architecture in genotype–phenotype mapping
  • Consequences of changing expression levels of biological systems (e.g. efflux pumps, metabolic genes)
Key papers
Lagator et al. 2022, eLife
Lagator et al. 2017, eLife
Lagator et al. 2015, Mol Biol Evol

02

Transcription factors

Transcription factors decide which genes are switched on, and when. We study how mutations change what they bind, and what that means for how regulatory networks can be rewired.

  • How mutations in transcription factors alter their binding preferences
  • Optimising binding preferences of transcription factors
  • Temperature sensitivity of transcription factors
Key papers
Igler et al. 2018, Nat Ecol Evol
Lagator et al. 2017, eLife

03

Intrinsic terminators

Terminators tell RNA polymerase where to stop. We measure how mutations change their function, and compare how they have evolved across species.

  • Mapping effects of mutations in terminators to their function
  • Evolution of intrinsic terminators

04

RNA polymerase and its mutations

RNA polymerase transcribes every gene, so a single mutation in it – including common antibiotic resistance mutations – can ripple across the whole cell. We follow those effects from protein structure all the way to fitness.

  • Mapping effects of RNA polymerase mutations across all relevant levels of biological complexity affected by them – protein structure, function, transcriptome and fitness
  • Developing transcriptional chassis carrying RNA polymerase mutations with defined expression profiles
Key papers
Ozbilek et al. 2025, Mol Biol Evol
Soley et al. 2023, Proc R Soc B

05

Plasmid–host interactions

Plasmids move genes such as antibiotic resistance between bacteria, but they also interfere with their hosts. We map how plasmid-borne regulators act on the host chromosome, and why carrying a plasmid is costly.

  • Transcriptional interactions between plasmid-borne transcription factors and the host chromosome
  • Genetic basis of fitness costs associated with plasmid carriage

06

Antimicrobial resistance evolution

Resistance evolves through mutations whose effects depend on the environment and on each other. We characterise resistance mutations at scale, and look at how human decisions shape how resistance spreads.

  • Characterising genomic mutations that lead to resistance and their dependence on environmental conditions
  • Role of epistasis in antimicrobial resistance evolution
  • Societal and industrial factors that contribute to the development of resistance
Key papers
Jago et al. 2025, Nat Commun
Ashfield et al. 2025, Microbiology
Lagator et al. 2021, Biol Lett

07

Mutational bias

Not all mutations are equally likely. We study how DNA repair, sequence context and genetic background bias which mutations arise, and what this means for adaptation.

  • How DNA repair activity, sequence context and genetic background shape which mutations arise
  • Measuring mutation rates and spectra across genotypes and environments
  • Consequences of mutational biases for adaptation, including antibiotic resistance
Key papers
Jago et al. 2026, PNAS
Green et al. 2025, Mol Biol Evol
