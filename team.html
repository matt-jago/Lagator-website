Dr Deniz Ozbilek

Postdoc

My research focuses on mapping the genotype-phenotype-fitness relationship of RNA polymerase in E. coli under fluctuating environmental conditions. By integrating structural, transcriptomic, and fitness-level analyses, we aim to uncover how mutations in RNA polymerase influence adaptation and compensate for resistance-associated fitness costs.

Xuan Chen

PhD student

My PhD project focuses on the genetic regulation of the lysis system in bacteriophage lambda, with particular emphasis on the transcriptional antiterminator Q protein and its role in phage ecology. My current work investigates how mutations in Q and epistatic interactions among its variants influence phage lysis regulation and fitness, linking these effects to patterns of natural genetic diversity. I am also interested in how Q-mediated antitermination activity varies across different genetic backgrounds.

Alya Tara Augustine

PhD student · October 2025–August 2026

My research interests lie in synthetic biology and thinking about ways we can reengineer and manipulate bacterial systems to give them new and useful functions. I am also interested in looking beyond the conventional model organisms to explore what less familiar bacteria have to offer. My master's thesis looked at the potential of Thermus thermophilus as a chassis for synthetic biology platforms. Ultimately, I am excited by the possibility of teaching old bacteria new tricks and new bacteria old ones!

Zhaopu Han

PhD student · Joined February 2026

I focus on the interdisciplinary research between biomaterial design and microbiology in orthopaedics. Scaffolds loaded with engineered probiotics are attempted to be introduced into the intervertebral discs to serve as sensors and reactors for key metabolites, which exert intervention on the pathological development of intervertebral disc degeneration. The long-term goal is to achieve the clinical translation in diagnosis and treatment.

Mary Yuhanna

PhD student · Joined October 2026

I'm interested in understanding how transcriptional regulatory systems coevolve in bacteriophages, particularly how mutations in regulatory DNA sequences and proteins influence each other's evolution. I'm using comparative genomics and computational modelling to explore these interactions, with the goal of understanding what drives and constrains the evolution of regulatory systems.

Hélène Zeller

PhD student · Joined September 2026

I'm interested in translational research and in understanding why certain phenotypes or genotypes are observed in specific contexts. My final-year project models human body fluids (urine, serum and the gut lumen) to ask how these environments shape de novo mutation generation in E. coli under antibiotic selection.
